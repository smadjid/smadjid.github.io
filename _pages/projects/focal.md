  <h2>Description</h2>

  <p>FOCAL is a diagnostic tool for assessing how well a STEM educational technology for blind and visually impaired (BVI) learners fits a given pairing of <strong>learning objective</strong> and <strong>educational context</strong>. It is built on a 4×34×3 matrix derived from a thematic analysis of 25 empirical studies, and applied through a 5-step decision protocol — the fourth step is deliberately eliminatory: a single missing essential feature is enough to flag a pedagogical risk, regardless of how strong the rest of the system is.</p>

  <h2>Main features</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Pedagogical diagnosis</strong> — identifies the primary learning objective (procedural fluency, conceptual understanding, analytical reasoning, or creative expression) and any secondary objectives.</li>
    <li><strong>Context mapping</strong> — situates the technology within one of three educational levels (elementary K–5, secondary 9–12, postsecondary) based on the dominant institutional constraint.</li>
    <li><strong>Automated matrix lookup</strong> — surfaces the essential features and adoption barrier specific to the selected objective–context cell.</li>
    <li><strong>Eliminatory evaluation</strong> — checks each essential feature; any single absence triggers a risk verdict.</li>
    <li><strong>Barrier mitigation check</strong> — independently evaluates the deployment plan (training, compatibility, funding) to ensure the identified barrier is actually addressed.</li>
    <li><strong>Diagnostic report generation</strong> — a full summary of every step, with a green/orange/red verdict and PDF export.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Status</h2>
  <p>Version 1.0 is publicly available. The tool was calibrated on the four illustrative cases from <em>From Diagnosis to Instrument</em> (Sadallah &amp; Encelle, 2026). A phase 2, pending ethical approval, plans anonymous telemetry to empirically validate the matrix's theoretical [T] cells.</p>
